Eight chromatic hues, eleven steps each, calibrated on APCA rather than on even lightness: the step numbers mean the same contrast job in every hue, so --red-600 and --blue-600 are interchangeable as a text-on-tint pair. Components never name a hue — they read --c-*, and hue-* or a status repoints that ramp at one of these.

Neutral ramp — the brand's own gray

50100200300400500600700800900950

One neutral, --gray-*, declared by the theme. It is both the surface family — paper, borders, ink — and what hue-gray points at, so a neutral tag matches the surface it sits on. There used to be a second: a warm pink-beige ramp shipped as the palette's ninth hue, unrelated to any theme's surfaces. Two spellings, two temperatures, one meaning; it is gone.

Core's is warm — the portfolio's oat paper, not a true gray — so every theme declares its own: a dashboard on oat renders cream, which is why Product ships a true neutral and Palette and Roadtrip ship cool dark ramps.

The chroma carries a curve rather than a flat value. It is felt in the light-mid tints, eased through the value mids where text legibility lives, and deepest in the shadows, so a border or a sunken fill sits in a colour instead of in a gray. The hue holds the paper's own family through 300 and only then walks toward the brand's forest green — saturation without an olive cast. Lightness is fixed by the APCA curve above, so tuning either one never moves the contrast.

Roles resolved from them

Object colour is never picked from a ramp directly. An emphasis class (emph-plain → emph-stark) resolves these seven roles at once from the current --c-* and --gray-*, and dark mode re-resolves them rather than inverting. The link blue is deliberately outside the accent ramp: the accent is green, and a link has to survive grayscale print and red-green colour blindness, so it carries both a distinct hue and an underline.
